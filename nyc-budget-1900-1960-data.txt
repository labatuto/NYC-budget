NEW YORK CITY BUDGET HISTORICAL DATA: 1900-1960
=================================================
Compiled from web research. All figures in nominal (current) dollars.
NYC Fiscal Year runs July 1 - June 30.


========================================================================
ERA 1: POST-CONSOLIDATION (1898-1914)
========================================================================

CIRCA 1899-1900:
- Total expenditures (1899): ~$68.5 million
  (Per capita: $19.56 x estimated population 3,500,000)
- Gross funded debt (Jan 1, 1900): $358,104,307.11
- Sinking fund: $105,435,871.70
- Net funded debt: $252,668,435.41
- Consolidation added ~$15 million/year in government costs over
  pre-consolidation combined expenditures
- Population (1900): ~3,437,000
SOURCE: Popular Science Monthly, Vol. 57, May 1900,
  "The Most Expensive City in the World"
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Popular_Science_Monthly/Volume_57/May_1900/The_Most_Expensive_City_in_the_World

1910:
- Total budget (appropriations): $163,130,270.37
SOURCE: 1922 Encyclopaedia Britannica, "New York (City)" entry
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1922_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/New_York_(City)

1910-1914 DETAILED DATA:
- Revenue and expenditure statements for 1910-1914 were compiled by
  NYC Department of Finance for the 1915 Constitutional Convention
- Document: "The City of New York: Summary and Detailed Statements of
  Revenues and Expenditures for the Years 1910 to 1914, Inclusive"
SOURCE: Internet Archive
  https://archive.org/details/cityofnewyorksum00newyuoft

PROPERTY & DEBT (1920, reflecting pre-war growth):
- Assessed taxable property (real + personal, 1920): $8,922,628,742
  - Real estate portion: $8,626,122,557
- Net funded debt (1920): $1,033,878,359
- Gross bonded debt: $1,238,260,597
- Floating debt: $42,350,400
- Sinking fund: $204,382,238
SOURCE: 1922 Encyclopaedia Britannica
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1922_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/New_York_(City)

ERA 3: GREAT DEPRESSION (1929-1939)
========================================================================

FISCAL CRISIS CONTEXT:
- NYC raised taxes 1930-1932 as revenue fell
- By 1932: half the city's factories closed; ~1/3 unemployed;
  1.6 million residents on relief
- Mayor's Committee (Oct 1930 - June 1932): raised $3,124,345,
  spent $2,930,566 on direct relief
- $8 million in public works wages spent; State Legislature
  appropriated $10 million more; Board of Aldermen approved
  $2 million in revenue bonds
- State income tax increased 50% by August 1931
- The regular city budget was "in the neighborhood of $600,000,000
  yearly" during the LaGuardia era (1937 analysis)

BANKER AGREEMENT (1933):
- Mayor O'Brien secured agreement with private banks to continue
  lending to city until 1937 in exchange for freezing real estate
  tax rates
- Earlier, a syndicate loaned the city $100,000,000 for 3 years at 6%

SOURCE: Marxists.org archive of James Burnham analysis (1937)
  https://www.marxists.org/history/etol/writers/burnham/1937/08/laguardia.htm
  New York Almanack: https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2023/09/great-depression-in-new-york-city/

LaGUARDIA BUDGET DATA (from Wikipedia - Mayoralty of Fiorello La Guardia):

  Fiscal Year  | Budget
  -------------|------------------
  1934         | Inherited $31 million deficit; reduced by $19 million
  1935         | $550,961,960.06
  1936         | $545,541,842
  1937         | $554,998,917.10
  1938         | $587,980,567
  1939         | $578,509,839

KEY ACTIONS:
- LaGuardia cut own salary in half
- Eliminated 1,000+ positions
- Consolidated five borough park departments into one
- Secured enabling legislation from Albany for special taxes
- Balanced budget by 1934, enabling federal funding applications
- NYC was single greatest beneficiary of WPA in entire U.S.

SOURCE: Wikipedia, "Mayoralty of Fiorello La Guardia"
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoralty_of_Fiorello_La_Guardia

MAYORS:
  James J. Walker: resigned Sept 1, 1932
  John P. O'Brien: Jan 1 - Dec 31, 1933
  Fiorello H. La Guardia: Jan 1, 1934 - Dec 31, 1945

ERA 5: POSTWAR EXPANSION (1946-1960)
========================================================================

O'DWYER ERA (1946-1950):
- Presided over NYC's first billion-dollar budget (late 1940s,
  exact year not confirmed but likely FY1948-49 or FY1949-50)
- Raised subway fare from 5 cents to 10 cents
- Allocated $22.5 million for parks (1946-1949)
- Public assistance recipients: ~300,000 at end of WWII

IMPELLITTERI ERA (1950-1953):
- Raised bus and subway fare to 15 cents
- Established parking meters for revenue
- Increased sales tax
- Fiscal pressures from postwar inflation

WAGNER ERA (1954-1965):
- Budget data points:
  * "Last pre-Wagner budget" (FY1953-54): implied ~$1.24 billion
    (derived from: Wagner's ~$3.09B budget was "nearly 2.5 times
    as big as the last pre-Wagner budget a decade ago")
  * By 1959: budget exceeded $2 billion
  * ~1963-64: $3,093,461,582 (biggest municipal budget in U.S.)
  * 1965 (Wagner's final budget): $3.87 billion
    (including $256 million in borrowing)

- Wagner sought $157 million in new revenue from Albany in 1959;
  State approved only $125 million
- Pay raises: planned $32 million, cut to $18.5 million
- Each of Wagner's 10 annual budgets exceeded the previous
- His final budget was 55% bigger than 5 years earlier
- 1957-1962: executive orders granting collective bargaining
  to city employee unions
- Third term (1962-65): spending up 30% (inflation-adjusted 33%)
- Last budget increase: ~$500 million (~15%), 5x inflation rate

WELFARE GROWTH:
  End of WWII: ~300,000 recipients of public assistance
  1965: over 500,000 recipients (2/3 increase; population flat)

SPENDING TREND:
  1930-1965: real per capita city spending increased ~350%

POPULATION:
  1940 Census: 7,454,995
  1950 Census: 7,891,957
  1960 Census: 7,781,984

KEY PRIMARY SOURCES FOR FURTHER RESEARCH
========================================================================

1. FRASER - Financial Statistics of Cities (Census Bureau, 1902-1940):
   Annual data on individual city revenues, expenditures, debt by function
   https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/financial-statistics-cities-164?browse=1900s
   Available years: 1905, 1906, 1907, 1908, 1909, 1910, 1911, 1912,
   1913, 1914, 1915, 1916, 1917, 1918, 1919, 1920, 1921, 1922, 1923,
   1924, 1925, 1926, 1927, 1928, 1929, 1930, 1931, 1932, 1933, 1934,
   1935, 1936, 1937, 1938
   ** THIS IS THE SINGLE BEST SOURCE for year-by-year NYC data with
   departmental breakdowns (police, fire, education, health, streets,
   charities, etc.) **

2. Internet Archive - NYC Dept of Finance (1910-1914 data):
   https://archive.org/details/cityofnewyorksum00newyuoft

3. HathiTrust - Annual Reports of NYC Comptroller:
   Digitized volumes for 1901, 1902, 1904, 1905, 1906, 1907, 1909, 1910+
   https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/011227103
   Also: 1909 "Comparative Tables" of budget appropriations since
   consolidation (1898-1909)

4. Census of Governments (1902, 1913, 1922, 1932, 1942, 1957):
   https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances/data/historical-data.html

5. NBER Working Paper 34011 by Pawel Janas:
   Municipal financial transactions for 700+ US cities, 1924-1943

6. NYC IBO Fiscal History (1985+):
   https://www.ibo.nyc.ny.us/fiscalhistory.html

7. LaGuardia & Wagner Archives at LaGuardia Community College:
   https://www.laguardiawagnerarchive.lagcc.cuny.edu/

8. NYC Municipal Archives:
   https://a860-collectionguides.nyc.gov/


========================================================================
NOTES ON DATA GAPS
========================================================================

- No year-by-year budget totals found online for: 1899-1909, 1911-1915,
  1917, 1919-1920, 1922-1923, 1927-1934, 1946-1958
- The FRASER "Financial Statistics of Cities" series (source #1 above)
  almost certainly contains this data for 1905-1938 but the PDFs could
  not be accessed during this research session
- The 1946-1960 gap is the hardest to fill; the Census Bureau's
  "City Government Finances" series (successor to Financial Statistics
  of Cities) covers 1942+ but is not easily accessible online
- Revenue vs. expenditure breakdowns and departmental data (education,
  police, fire, welfare, health, debt service) were not found in
  web-accessible sources but exist in the FRASER/Census publications
